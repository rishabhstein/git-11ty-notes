Reaction Precipitation fingers

Posted 25 Oct 2025 Modified 02 Jan 2026, 12:54

Fingering patterns are not uncommon in reactive systems, especially in chemically dissolving ones (see dissolution fingers). However, it is not obvious whether a similar chemically precipitating system can produce patterns?

In a system where a reaction of (A+B->C) type is involved, it has been shown experimentally (Cobalt chloride injected in silicate) that a rich variety of patterns, aka chemical garden, can form [1, 2]. These patterns resemble plant-like mineral forms, which share properties with many self-organising systems, such as nanoscale tubes, corrosion filaments and chimneys at hydrothermal vents, at different length scales. A particular form of pattern is phyllotactic-pattern where logarithmic spiral appears in a reaction-precipitation experiments.

Interestingly, this gives a big hope for CO2\rm CO_2CO2​ sequestration process as understanding precipitation fingers can help us in finding an optimal condition for maximum CO2\rm CO_2CO2​ trapping in rocks.

Gabor and De Wit [2] showed that in Hele-Shaw cell injecting aqueous solution of carbonate in aqueous solution of Ca++\rm Ca^{++}Ca++ produces precipitating patterns, and with large concentration and high flow rates the amount of precipitate drops significantly.
