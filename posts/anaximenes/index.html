Anaximenes

Posted 04 Oct 2024 Modified 10 Nov 2025, 19:01

Contents
  1. (Top)
  2. References

He was from Miletus—a town in present day Turkey—and probably a contemporary of both Thales and Anaximander. However, his belief was more close to Thales. He claimed:

Source of all things is air (or vapor 1).

Water exists in air until pressed out. At higher pressure it convert to water and then later to ice.

If he has existed at the time of Thales, this claim must have lead to a big rivalry. Or he could be a student of Thales who just tried to follow the path of his guru and found that air/vapor is more fundamental to water?

Assuming air/vapor as a fundamental thing leads to more questions, such as "What is air made of?" or "Where does air exist—it need also a place?".

I can imagine that living in older times we could only see fundamental elements such as air, fire, and water. However, other cultures such as ancient Hindus added earth and vaccum (Akasha) to this list of fundamental things.
