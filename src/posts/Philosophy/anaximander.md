Another philosopher from Miletus—a town in present day Turkey—who ~~could be~~ was [2](#2) a contemporary of [Thales of Miletus](thalesofmiletus) and [Anaximenes](anaximenes). His claim is rather bizarre and completely unrelated to the other two Miletian philosophers:

> Our world is one of the myriads of the worlds that evolve and dissolve in boundless [1](#1)

Now it is unclear what does he mean from boundless? As a layman, I would see this statement as a spiritual one where beings come and go in this world. On the other hand, in terms of matter, he might be inspired from physical phenomenons. For instance a wood burns, turns into charcoal and releases heat—one world (matter) into another (energy).

Unfortunately there is no surviving document that describes what *Anaximander*'s boundless is. [Aristotle](aristotle) interpreted it like modern day ether [2](#2) 

Perhaps he meant something fundamental of which the world is made of, in which before and after the existence the world dissolves into.
