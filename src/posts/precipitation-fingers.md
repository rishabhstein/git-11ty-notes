Fingering patterns are not uncommon in reactive systems, especially in chemically dissolving ones (see [dissolution fingers](dissolution-fingers.md)). However, it is not obvious whether a similar chemically precipitating system can produce patterns?

In a system where a reaction of (A+B->C) type is involved, it has been shown experimentally (Cobalt chloride injected in silicate) that a rich variety of patterns, aka *chemical garden*, can form [^1] [^2]. These patterns resemble plant-like mineral forms, which share properties with many self-organising systems, such as nanoscale tubes, corrosion filaments and chimneys at hydrothermal vents,  at different length scales. A particular form of pattern is [phyllotactic-pattern](phyllotactic-pattern.md) where logarithmic spiral appears in a reaction-precipitation experiments.

Interestingly, this gives a ==big hope for $\rm CO_2$ sequestration process== as understanding precipitation fingers can help us in finding an optimal condition for maximum $\rm CO_2$ trapping in rocks. 

Gabor and De Wit [^2]  showed that in Hele-Shaw cell injecting aqueous solution of carbonate in aqueous solution of $\rm Ca^{++}$ produces precipitating patterns, and with large concentration and high flow rates the amount of precipitate drops significantly.
