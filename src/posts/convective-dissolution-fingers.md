Important for $\text{CO}_2$sequestration process, which is quite popular nowadays. 

The nomenclature is quiet confusing so *not to be confused by [dissolution (chemical erosion) fingers](dissolution-fingers.md)*.

These fingers are a result of a buoyancy driven process where injection of a gas, such as $\text{CO}_2$, in salt water results an increase in density of the brine solution. Denser brine sinks and eventually is replaced by lighter brine (with $\text{CO}_2$). This process gives rise to the fingering pattern[^1]

The above explanation indicates that these fingers are equivalent to [Rayleigh-Taylor fingers](rtfingers.md). However,  De Wit points out [^1] that former are **theoretically different** than the latter, especially for miscible fluids because $\text{CO}_2$ is only partially miscible.

#### Reactive system
Considering now the case when gaseous phase can *chemically react* with liquid phase. Let us consider A + B -> C case. $\text{CO}_2$ can either increase or decrease the density of host liquid phase . For example, in case of $\text{CO}_2 +\rm KOH$ case the product has higher density than the reactant, compared to the case of $\text{CO}_2 + \rm NaOH$ where the product is less dense than reactant.
