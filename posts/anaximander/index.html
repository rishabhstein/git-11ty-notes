Anaximander

Posted 04 Oct 2024 Modified 10 Nov 2025, 19:01

Contents
  1. (Top)
  2. References

Another philosopher from Miletus—a town in present day Turkey—who could be was 2 a contemporary of Thales of Miletus and Anaximenes. His claim is rather bizarre and completely unrelated to the other two Miletian philosophers:

Our world is one of the myriads of the worlds that evolve and dissolve in boundless 1

Now it is unclear what does he mean from boundless? As a layman, I would see this statement as a spiritual one where beings come and go in this world. On the other hand, in terms of matter, he might be inspired from physical phenomenons. For instance a wood burns, turns into charcoal and releases heat—one world (matter) into another (energy).

Unfortunately there is no surviving document that describes what Anaximander's boundless is. Aristotle interpreted it like modern day ether 2

Perhaps he meant something fundamental of which the world is made of, in which before and after the existence the world dissolves into.
