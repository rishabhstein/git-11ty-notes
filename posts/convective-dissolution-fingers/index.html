Convective dissolution fingers

Important for $\text{CO}_2$sequestration process, which is quite popular nowadays.

The nomenclature is quiet confusing so not to be confused by dissolution (chemical erosion) fingers.

These fingers are a result of a buoyancy driven process where injection of a gas, such as CO2\text{CO}_2CO2​, in salt water results an increase in density of the brine solution. Denser brine sinks and eventually is replaced by lighter brine (with CO2\text{CO}_2CO2​). This process gives rise to the fingering pattern1

The above explanation indicates that these fingers are equivalent to Rayleigh-Taylor fingers. However, De Wit points out 1:1 that former are theoretically different than the latter, especially for miscible fluids because CO2\text{CO}_2CO2​ is only partially miscible.

Reactive system

Considering now the case when gaseous phase can chemically react with liquid phase. Let us consider A + B -> C case. CO2\text{CO}_2CO2​ can either increase or decrease the density of host liquid phase . For example, in case of CO2+KOH\text{CO}_2 +\rm KOHCO2​+KOH case the product has higher density than the reactant, compared to the case of CO2+NaOH\text{CO}_2 + \rm NaOHCO2​+NaOH where the product is less dense than reactant.
