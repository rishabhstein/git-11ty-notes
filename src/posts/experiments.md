Discussion with Norbi about using SnCl2:
1. SnCl2 is not dissoluble in water, and needs acid to be dissolved. This makes it complicated to use for phyllotaxy experiments. GPT said the same.
2. **Alternative can be SnNO3**
3. Nitrates are good alternative of Chlorides as they are highly soluble in water.
4. Other salt in the range of density BaCO3 to SrCO3 are:

| Compound | Formula | Approx. Density (g/cm³) | Chemical Garden Suitability | Notes |
|---|---|---:|---|---|
| Barium carbonate | BaCO₃ | 4.3–4.5 | Excellent | Fast precipitation, strong tubes, toxic Ba²⁺ |
| Strontium carbonate | SrCO₃ | 3.5–3.8 | Excellent | Stable membranes, reliable growth |
| Zinc carbonate | ZnCO₃ | ~4.4 | Very Good | Dense, rapid precipitation, good substitute for BaCO₃ |
| Iron(II) carbonate | FeCO₃ | ~3.9 | Good | Can oxidize in air, darker precipitates |
| Manganese carbonate | MnCO₃ | ~3.7 | Good | Moderate stability, useful density range |
| Calcium carbonate | CaCO₃ | 2.7–2.9 | Very Good | Fast precipitation but lower density |
| Tin(II) carbonate* | SnCO₃ | poorly defined | Fair/Poor | Hydrolyzes easily, gelatinous precipitates |
| Cadmium carbonate | CdCO₃ | ~4.3 | Good physically | Highly toxic, generally not recommended |
| Lead carbonate | PbCO₃ | ~6.6 | Moderate | Very dense but highly toxic |
| Magnesium carbonate | MgCO₃ | ~3.0 | Fair | More soluble, weaker precipitation |
