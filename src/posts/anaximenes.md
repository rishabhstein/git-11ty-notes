He was from Miletus—a town in present day Turkey—and probably a contemporary of both [Thales](thalesofmiletus) and [Anaximander](anaximander). However, his belief was more close to [Thales](thalesofmiletus). He claimed:

> Source of all things is *air* (or *vapor* ^[[1](#1)]). 

Water exists in air until pressed out. At higher pressure it convert to water and then later to ice. 

If he has existed at the time of [Thales](thalesofmiletus), this claim must have lead to a big rivalry. Or he could be a student of [Thales](thalesofmiletus) who just tried to follow the path of his guru and found that *air/vapor* is more fundamental to *water*?

Assuming *air/vapor* as a fundamental thing leads to more questions, such as *"What is air made of?"* or *"Where does air exist—it need also a place?"*.

I can imagine that living in older times we could only see fundamental elements such as *air, fire, and water*. However, other cultures such as [ancient *Hindus*](indianphilosophy)  added *earth and vaccum (Akasha)* to this list of fundamental things.
