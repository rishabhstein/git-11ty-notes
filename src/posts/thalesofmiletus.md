A *pre-Socratic* Greek philosopher from Miletus—a town of present day Turkey—(existed around the same time as [Anaximenes](anaximenes) and [Anaximander](anaximander)). He is often referred as **the first philosopher** who claimed:

 > Source of all things is water. 

Both statements are vague. The former assumes there were no philosophers before him, which seems unlikely. What about philosophers from other cultures, such as Tao, [Charvaka](charvaka), [Ajivika](ajivika) and other unknown philosophers? The second statement doesn’t explain what does it mean that "source of all things is water". 

Are we made of water? Is all matter just water? These questions further complicates things and lead to more questions such as What is this water made of? What about atoms? [Democritus](democritus) from Greece and [Kanada](kanada) from India—as far as I remember after Thales—proposed the idea of atoms, independently.

He was an extensive traveller and said to have calculated the height of pyramids in Egypt by measuring shadows around 585 B.C. [1](#1)
