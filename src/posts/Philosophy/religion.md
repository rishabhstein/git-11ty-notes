# God
It is important to first define what is God? The context of this word is [[Philosophy/religion]] where the meaning is different based on different religions. Starting from prehistoric era about which we know a very little, my best guess for the definition of God comes from animal, nature, life and death. In most of the cave paintings the common figures around the world are half human half beast forms. Fear of death and appreciation of live gives second candidate. The ancient burial ground indicates that there was some belief. Worshiping a figurine of a fertile woman, goddess of fertility, was also common practice. I found a similar figurine in a museum exhibition in Bulgaria where artifacts of ten thousand year old society were shown. They had only two Gods: fertile mother and Sun (priest) as father.

_So does that mean older the religion, closer it will be to the nature or animals? Less human-centric?_

## Religion
In modern religions, such as Abrahamic religion, God is a superhuman figure who created the world, and still governs it and intervenes in our life. While in vedic religion there is not one but multiple Gods in hierarchies such as Brahma, Vishnu and Shiva, and the demigods: wind god, fire god, earth goddess, and so on. These gods also intervene in human society, and the supernatural theme is also common. Its a surprise that no one thought about animals in human religion except using them as tools. Both monotheist and polytheist religions only concerns with human matters.

Then there are religions where natural processes are godly but there is no God. You can only perceive Him through his creations. Japan’s Shintoism and India’s Jainism are few examples.

Lastly comes the category of religions where there is no supernatural or divine creations at all. They are focused on practicing [[philosophy]]. Buddhism is the most known example.

## Spirituality
This overlaps a lot with religion, however with a clear distinction of believing in *spirit or atma*. Not all religion accepts the existence of individual’s spirit.
